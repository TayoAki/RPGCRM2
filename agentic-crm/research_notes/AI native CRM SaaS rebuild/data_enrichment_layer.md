# B2B Data and Enrichment Layer for AI-Native CRMs (as of 2026-11-10)

Scope: people/company data APIs, contact-finding and waterfall enrichment, email verification, AI web research APIs, signals/intent, local/SMB data; pricing, resale/embedding rights, CRM wiring, compliance; build-vs-buy for a small multi-tenant AI CRM. The CRM's current stack: a LinkedIn post-search/engager service, Hunter + Apollo (emails), an email verifier, Firecrawl/Exa/Parallel (web research), Google Places (local), and SAM.gov (government contracting).

Source quality note: most third-party pricing figures come from vendor-adjacent blogs (Prospeo, Abmatic, MarketBetter, Parallel, Crustdata, etc. all sell competing products). Primary pricing pages and docs were fetched where possible and are marked "(primary)". Prices fetched in Nov 2026 unless dated otherwise.

## 1. Price per enriched person / company / email / phone at small and medium volume; minimums and contracts (with coverage, data types, API)

### Takeaway
Verified emails now cost roughly $0.01–$0.06 each at small volume from self-serve finders (LeadMagic, Hunter, FullEnrich, Findymail), mobile phones $0.04–$0.55, verification $0.004–$0.02, and full person/company records $0.03–$0.28 (PDL, Crustdata, Diffbot, Explorium). The premium "big data" vendors (ZoomInfo, Bombora, Harmonic, UserGems, Warmly) remain annual-contract, $10K–$50K+ entry. AI search/research APIs (Exa, Parallel, Firecrawl) are now cheap enough ($0.001–$0.01 per call) to be the default research layer, and two of them (Exa Agent, Firecrawl "Alexandria") have started bundling contact-enrichment data directly.

### Inferences
- Unit-cost ladder at small volume (≤10K/mo), derived from the primary pages above: work email found ≈ $0.009–$0.02 (LeadMagic/Hunter annual) → $0.03 (Apollo match via Firecrawl) → $0.05 (FullEnrich/Findymail) ; mobile ≈ $0.04–$0.10 (LeadMagic) → $0.07 (Exa Agent) → $0.49–$0.55 (Findymail/FullEnrich waterfall); verification ≈ $0.004 (MillionVerifier) – $0.02 (ZeroBounce PAYG); person/company record ≈ $0.03 (Diffbot, Explorium Starter, Crustdata likely) – $0.28 (PDL Pro list).
- Phones dominate cost: one mobile ≈ 5–10 email lookups everywhere (Apollo +8, LeadMagic 5, FullEnrich/Findymail/BetterContact 10). Fetch phones only on explicit user action.
- Self-serve minimums are small ($19–$99/mo); real minimums appear only for licensed/embedded use and premium intent/job-change data ($10K–$150K/yr).
- AI search APIs are converging with enrichment vendors (Exa Agent email/phone enrichment and Exa Connect; Firecrawl Alexandria fronting Apollo/PDL/FullEnrich; Crustdata adding web search/fetch). A small CRM can increasingly buy "research + contact" through one metered API — but the resale terms of the underlying data still flow through (see Q2).
- Google Places Enterprise SKUs (where phone/website/ratings live, per Google's SKU structure — not confirmed on the fetched page) cost ~$20–$35 per 1K, i.e., 2–3.5¢ per business, roughly the price of an email lookup.

## 2. Can a SaaS resell or embed the data to its own customers? Which providers are safe for a multi-tenant product?

### Takeaway
Default self-serve/API terms of the big data vendors (Apollo, PDL, ZoomInfo, Clay, HubSpot) license data for the buyer's **internal business use only**; showing it to your own customers requires a separate reseller/embedded/OEM agreement. A few contact-finder vendors explicitly sell embed/reseller terms (Apollo Data Reseller program, FullEnrich Enterprise, Findymail credit plans, Explorium custom plans). For a multi-tenant CRM the two safe patterns are (a) BYOK — each tenant licenses the data with its own key — or (b) a signed reseller/embedded agreement.

### Cited Findings
- **Apollo API Terms (updated 2024-08-13)**: users may not sublicense, sell, or distribute the APIs; may not use an API to replicate or compete with Apollo (Apollo's sole discretion); may not sell/sublicense API access to third parties. — [Apollo API terms](https://www.apollo.io/terms/api)
- **Apollo Data Reseller Program**: "Embed industry-leading B2B data into your product" / "Turn Apollo's premium data into new offerings and upsells"; endpoints: people & org enrichment, org & people search, job postings, news; process: consult → trial key → sign API Reseller agreement ("most partners complete… within a week") → launch; pricing/minimums not published; white-label not mentioned. — [Apollo API reseller page](https://www.apollo.io/partners/api-reseller)
- **Apollo data via Firecrawl (Schedule F, effective 2026-10-07)**: allowed only for the customer's internal business purposes; prohibited: distributing/reselling/sublicensing/publishing "outside your organization, including through products, datasets, APIs, exports, or agent outputs visible to outsiders"; no AI/ML training; no competing product; customer is independent controller; must delete on forwarded opt-out/deletion requests (survives termination); acceptance record shared with Apollo. — [Firecrawl third-party data terms – Apollo](https://www.firecrawl.dev/terms/third-party-data/apollo/F-1.0.2)
- **PDL**: data license feeds and on-prem/flat-file delivery are Enterprise-only; ToS/Acceptable Data Use Policy linked but resale/display rules not on pricing page. — [PDL pricing](https://www.peopledatalabs.com/pricing). Downstream PDL schedules forbid certain verticals (guns, adult, credit repair, payday loans, tobacco/marijuana) without PDL authorization and FCRA uses; ADUP breach = material breach. — [Firecrawl PDL schedule (2026-10-20)](https://www.firecrawl.dev/terms/third-party-data/people-data-labs/I-1.1.0); [Primer third-party terms](https://www.sayprimer.com/third-party-terms)

Claims Apollo prohibits by default, ZoomInfo requires custom negotiation, PDL needs enterprise tier, Clearbit tied to HubSpot, Explorium offers resale rights on custom plans covering 50+ sources under one DPA; OEM/reseller licenses "often require $50K+ minimums and a 6–12 month negotiation". — [Explorium blog](https://www.explorium.ai/blog/data-for-gtm/b2b-data-api-resale-rights-licensing-what-product-builders-need-to-know-now-in-2026.md)

- **Findymail**: "Credit-based plans are for reselling or embedding data. Usage-based plans are for use within your own team." — [Findymail pricing](https://www.findymail.com/pricing/)
- **Clay**: "We never white-label or overcharge you to use data providers on Clay"; Integrations Partner Program is listing/co-marketing, not white-label; Clay API is Enterprise-only. — [Clay integrations partner program](https://www.clay.com/partners/integrations); [Clay pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing)
- **ZoomInfo**: per a single secondary source, an amendment notified 2026-02-27 lets ZoomInfo reuse data customers send via integrations (e.g., CRM) under a perpetual licence (unverified). — [emelia.io](https://emelia.io/software/zoominfo/pricing)
- **HubSpot Breeze Intelligence** only available inside a paid HubSpot subscription (no standalone API for new customers). — [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/how-does-clearbit-work)

- **Hunter, LeadMagic, Crustdata**: their pricing/docs pages state no resale or embedding terms either way. — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/pricing); [LeadMagic](https://leadmagic.io/pricing); [Crustdata docs](https://docs.crustdata.com/general/pricing.md)

### Inferences
- The CRM's current pattern (its own Hunter/Apollo keys serving all tenants) most likely falls under "internal business purposes" licenses; serving tenants from one key is redistribution outside the licensee's organization. Apollo's terms, plus the Firecrawl flow-down language ("agent outputs visible to outsiders"), make this explicit for Apollo. Treat this as the top licensing risk to fix.
- Multi-tenant-safe options, ranked by effort: (1) **BYOK per tenant** (tenant pastes its own Apollo/Hunter/FullEnrich/Clay key; tenant is licensee and controller; the CRM is a processor) — cheapest and fastest; (2) **vendors with published embed terms** (Findymail credit plans, FullEnrich Enterprise reseller with sub-accounts, Apollo Data Reseller agreement, Explorium custom) — sign before metering credits to tenants; (3) **OEM data licenses** (PDL Enterprise, ZoomInfo, Coresignal datasets) — $50K+ and months of negotiation, not for a small SaaS yet.
- Web research outputs (Exa/Parallel/Firecrawl scraping of public web pages) are generally a different licensing posture than licensed contact databases, but Firecrawl Alexandria/Exa Connect provider calls inherit the underlying provider's restrictions.
- Storing Google Places phone/website/rating in CRM records long-term likely conflicts with Google's caching terms; store place_id and re-fetch on view, or use a source with storage rights (needs legal confirmation).
- Clay cannot be embedded; it can only be a tenant-owned upstream that pushes into the CRM via webhooks/HTTP API (Growth plan, $495/mo, on the tenant's account).

### Gaps
- Actual Apollo reseller pricing/minimums; PDL ADUP text on end-user display; Hunter, LeadMagic, Prospeo, Crustdata, Exa, Parallel ToS clauses on embedding (not fetched).
- Current Google Places API Policies page text on caching (only archived service terms and a secondary summary found).

## 4. Compliance: GDPR/CCPA, data subject requests, opt-out, LinkedIn scraping risk

### Takeaway
LinkedIn wins every recent scraping fight that involves fake/logged-in accounts (hiQ 2022, Proxycurl 2025, ProAPIs 2026), while logged-out scraping of public data survived Meta v. Bright Data (2024). In the EU, the CNIL fined Kaspr €240K (Dec 2024) for collecting LinkedIn contacts users had hidden. In California, data brokers must process the DROP deletion feed every 45 days from 2026-08-01. Data vendors now flow deletion/opt-out obligations down to API customers, so the CRM needs a suppression and deletion-propagation pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- **hiQ v. LinkedIn**: 2022-11-04 summary judgment for LinkedIn on breach of contract (scraping + fake profiles violated the User Agreement); 2022-12-08 consent judgment and permanent injunction: $500,000, stop scraping, destroy code/data/algorithms derived from scraped data. — [Morgan Lewis (Dec 2022)](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/12/linkedin-v-hiq-landmark-data-scraping-suit-provides-guidance-to-data-scrapers-and-web-operators); [Proskauer](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/2022/12/08/hiq-and-linkedin-reach-proposed-settlement-in-landmark-scraping-case/)
- **Meta v. Bright Data**: 2024-01-23, N.D. Cal. (Judge Chen) — Facebook/Instagram terms "only prohibit logged-in scraping, and not logged-off scraping"; Meta later waived appeal/abandoned the suit. — [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/technology/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/393996/None)
- **Proxycurl**: sued Jan 2025 (fake accounts, scraping millions of profiles), shut 2025-07-04 to comply with settlement. — [Nubela](https://nubela.co/blog/goodbye-proxycurl/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wins-legal-case-data-scrapers-proxycurl/756101/)
- **ProAPIs/Netswift**: sued Oct 2025 (millions of fake accounts, data sold up to $15K/month); consent judgment reported 2026-09-21: stop mass scraping, stop selling/transferring data, stop fake accounts, delete scraped data. LinkedIn also won against an unnamed browser-extension scraping firm ~5 months earlier. — [The Record](https://therecord.media/linkedin-wins-court-order-blocking-mass-scraping); [Security Affairs](https://securityaffairs.com/183001/security/linkedin-sues-proapis-for-15k-month-linkedin-data-scraping-scheme.html)
- **CNIL v. Kaspr**: €240,000 fine (decided 2024-12-05, published 2024-12-19) — no legal basis for collecting contacts of LinkedIn users who restricted visibility (Art. 6), 5-year retention (Art. 5), transparency failures (Arts. 12, 14), inadequate access responses (Art. 15: saying data came "from publicly accessible sources" insufficient); ordered deletion, six months to comply. — [CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/en/data-scraping-kaspr-fined-eu240000); [EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/data-scraping-french-supervisory-authority-fined-kaspr-eu240-000_de)
- **California Delete Act / DROP**: from 2026-08-01 registered data brokers must access DROP at least every 45 days and process deletions (also to service providers/contractors); $200 per deletion request per day fines; SB 361 doubled the registration fine; independent audits every 3 years from 2028-01-01. A "data broker" is a business that knowingly collects and sells personal information of consumers with whom it has no direct relationship. — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publication/dont-drop-ball-upcoming-changes-california-delete-act-five-key-steps-companies); [Alston & Bird](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/drop-is-coming-due-what-californias-delete-act-means-for-data-brokers-in-august/); [TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/resource/california-delete-act-drop-platform-data-brokers/)
- **Contractual flow-down**: Apollo-via-Firecrawl makes the customer an independent controller responsible for notices/consents and requires prompt deletion upon forwarded opt-out/deletion requests, surviving termination; PDL data excluded from FCRA uses. — [Firecrawl Apollo schedule](https://www.firecrawl.dev/terms/third-party-data/apollo/F-1.0.2); [Primer terms](https://www.sayprimer.com/third-party-terms)
- Account-based LinkedIn APIs (Unipile, Linked API) shift ToS risk to the end user's own LinkedIn account; dataset vendors carry injunction/deletion continuity risk. (Vendor page.) — [Linked API guide](https://linkedapi.io/guides/proxycurl-alternatives.md)

### Inferences
- The CRM's LinkedIn post-search/engager service is the highest legal/continuity risk in the stack: if it relies on fake or pooled logged-in accounts it matches the Proxycurl/ProAPIs fact pattern; if it uses the tenant's own session it shifts ban risk to the tenant. The provider's sourcing model should be confirmed; Crustdata's social-post endpoints (dataset + live, Enterprise) are an alternative with the risk held by a vendor, though dataset vendors can also be enjoined.
- Kaspr shows EU regulators treat "it was public on LinkedIn" as insufficient when users restricted visibility, and require source disclosure on access requests — the CRM should store provenance (provider + date) per enriched field to answer Art. 15/CCPA requests.
- A CRM that only enriches on behalf of each tenant (tenant as controller, CRM as processor/service provider) is less likely to be a "data broker"; a CRM that builds a shared cross-tenant contact cache and serves it to other tenants starts to look like one (DROP registration, 45-day deletion). Keep enrichment caches tenant-scoped. (Legal review needed.)
- Minimum compliance plumbing: global suppression list, per-field provenance, deletion propagation from vendor notices and DSARs, retention limits on unused enriched contacts, and a block on enrichment for regulated uses (FCRA-type decisions).

### Gaps
- Enforcement actions under DROP since 2026-08-01 (none found).
- Terms of the CRM's specific LinkedIn engager vendor (not named in the brief).
- GDPR legitimate-interest guidance for ordinary, publicly visible B2B contact data beyond the Kaspr case.
- SAM.gov data terms were not researched (public government data; out of scope here).

## 5. Recommendation-ready facts: best-value waterfall for a small SaaS; build vs buy

### Takeaway
For a small multi-tenant AI CRM the best value is: cheap first-pass email finder (LeadMagic or Hunter at ~1–2¢), fallback to an aggregator waterfall (FullEnrich/BetterContact) only on misses, verify everything with a sub-cent verifier, and fetch phones only on demand. Keep Exa/Parallel/Firecrawl as the agent research layer (they are cheap and now partially cover contact data). Build the orchestration, caching, metering and compliance; buy the data; do not build LinkedIn scraping.

### Cited Findings
- Cheapest first-pass email: LeadMagic 1 credit/email at $0.0085–$0.0198/credit; Hunter 1 credit/found email at ~$0.0084–$0.0245/credit; both charge only on found/valid results. — [LeadMagic](https://leadmagic.io/pricing); [Hunter](https://hunter.io/pricing)

### Inferences
- **Example per-contact cost** for "find + verify work email" with LeadMagic first (assume ~60% hit), FullEnrich on misses, MillionVerifier on all: ≈ 0.6×$0.02 + 0.4×$0.055 + $0.004 ≈ $0.038 per attempted contact at entry plans; ≈ $0.02 at LeadMagic Ultimate/FullEnrich volume. Adding a mobile for 1 in 10 contacts adds ~$0.01–$0.05. (Hit rates are assumptions, not sourced.)
- **Recommended stack changes vs current CRM**: keep Hunter or swap to LeadMagic as first pass; add FullEnrich (or BetterContact) as the waterfall fallback rather than calling Apollo per tenant from a shared key; keep the verifier (MillionVerifier is cheapest); keep Exa + Parallel (FindAll for list building, Task for research), Firecrawl for page extraction; keep Google Places but store only place_id plus user-confirmed fields; re-evaluate the LinkedIn engager vendor's sourcing model.
- **Licensing route**: Phase 1 BYOK for Apollo/Hunter/Clay (tenant keys, zero license exposure, tenant pays vendor); Phase 2 bundled credits only through vendors with written embed/reseller terms (Findymail credit plans, FullEnrich Enterprise reseller, Apollo Data Reseller, Explorium custom) and pass costs through a CRM-level credit meter.
- **Build**: provider-agnostic enrichment router (waterfall order per field, stop-on-hit, budget caps per tenant/job), tenant-scoped cache with TTL and provenance, credit metering/billing, suppression & deletion propagation, agent tools/MCP for "enrich", "find accounts", "research company". **Buy**: contact data, verification, firmographics, web search/extraction, visitor ID and intent. **Avoid building**: LinkedIn scraping or account pools, Google Maps scraping, a shared cross-tenant people database (data-broker exposure).
- Vendor concentration risk: several cheap vendors are small and change pricing often (Clay March 2026, Explorium Sept 2026, Exa June–July 2026, UserGems Aug 2026); the router should make providers swappable by config.

### Gaps
- No neutral benchmark of match/accuracy rates by provider for the CRM's ICP; a 200–500 contact bake-off is the only reliable way to set waterfall order.
- Reseller pricing (Apollo, FullEnrich, Explorium) requires sales conversations.
- Exa Websets vs Exa Agent pricing for list building may shift (Websets docs steer to Agent).
