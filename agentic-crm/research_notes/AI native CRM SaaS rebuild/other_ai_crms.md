# The wider AI-native CRM and AI revenue-agent field (excluding Attio, Lightfield, Day AI), plus incumbent responses — as of 2026-11-10

Scope note: research done 2026-11-10. Many third-party pages carry 2026 dates on older content; where a fact's date is uncertain it is flagged. "Vendor-claimed" means the only source is the company or its marketing. Several trade pages (pulse.bot, aggregator blogs, competitor blogs such as coffee.ai, 11x.ai, artisan.co, warmly.ai) are biased or low quality; they are used only where nothing better was found and are flagged.

## Q1. Product-by-product: features, agents, autonomy/approval model, data capture, enrichment, integrations/API/MCP, pricing and AI metering

### Takeaway
The field has split into (a) full AI-native CRMs that replace the system of record (Clarify, Monaco, Reevo, Zero, folk, Twenty), (b) agent layers that sit on top of Salesforce/HubSpot/warehouse (Rox, Actively, Unify, Common Room), and (c) incumbents bolting agents onto their CRMs (Agentforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho). Almost everyone now meters AI with credits or "actions" on top of a flat or per-seat fee, captures email/calendar/meetings automatically, and exposes MCP; the common approval pattern is "internal writes autonomous, anything that reaches a customer needs approval."

### Inferences
- The modal 2026 pricing shape for AI-native challengers is "flat platform fee or cheap seat + metered AI credits/actions, unlimited seats" (Clarify, Rox Teams, Reevo per-org pools, Unify credits, Twenty workspace credits). Incumbents meter per action/outcome (Salesforce $0.10/action, HubSpot $0.50/resolution, $1/qualified lead). A Bot product charging a flat per-workspace plan with bounded AI is in line with the challengers, but buyers now expect a visible usage meter, alerts, caps and per-feature toggles (Clarify, Twenty and Rox all ship these).
- The "autonomous internal, approval for external" boundary (Zero states it explicitly; Monaco's human AEs, Rox stage-gating approvals, HubSpot's step check-ins) is now the de-facto norm, so outreach-with-human-approval is table stakes rather than a differentiator; the differentiator is how good and fast the approval UX is, and where it happens.
- Messaging the agent from outside the app is rare and mostly limited to Slack (Rox Slack DMs, Common Room/Clarify Slack integrations) and Microsoft Teams/Copilot (Rox, Twenty's Teams transcript app). I found no notable AI-native CRM offering Telegram or SMS as a first-class channel to its agent; the only Telegram/WhatsApp CRM-agent offerings found were freelance builds and small vertical tools. This is a genuine (if niche) differentiator for an SMB/owner-operator audience, but it is an inference from absence, not a confirmed market gap.

## Q2. Architecture and build details (stack, warehouse-native designs, multi-tenancy, LLM providers, agent frameworks)

### Takeaway
Public architecture detail is thin; Rox is the only challenger with a documented distinctive architecture (warehouse-native knowledge graph, agent swarm, OpenAI + Bedrock). Twenty is the only one whose stack is fully inspectable (NestJS/Postgres, Vercel AI SDK with six provider families, cron/database-event agent triggers). MCP and public APIs/llms.txt are now standard surfaces.

### Inferences
- Two build philosophies: "replace the system of record and own the data model" (Clarify, Monaco, Reevo, Zero, Twenty, folk) vs "sit on the customer's existing CRM/warehouse" (Rox, Actively, Unify, Common Room). Warehouse-native is an enterprise play (Snowflake/Databricks customers, in-VPC, BYOK); for SMB/owner-operator buyers it is irrelevant, and owning a single complete record (Zero's argument) is the simpler path.
- Multi-model routing with visible cost (Twenty's tiered picker, Rox metering "by model type") suggests per-task model selection and cost attribution are becoming standard engineering practice; single-provider lock-in is uncommon among challengers.
- Scheduled/triggered agents (Twenty cron + database-event triggers, Rox agent triggers/monitoring, HubSpot Agent Builder triggers) mean "scheduled routines" are a baseline capability, not a differentiator.

### Gaps
- No public detail on multi-tenancy design for Clarify, Monaco, Reevo, Zero, Actively (shared vs isolated DBs).
- LLM providers unknown for Clarify, Monaco, Reevo, Zero, Actively, folk (folk uses Perplexity for Research assistant per secondary source only), Affinity.
- Agent frameworks (LangGraph, custom, etc.) not disclosed by any challenger except Rox's in-house "Agent Swarm" framework.

## Q4. Synthesis: table stakes vs differentiators, and user complaints

### Takeaway
By late 2026, automatic email/calendar/meeting capture, AI record updates, meeting prep/recaps, drafted follow-ups, built-in enrichment, an AI chat over CRM data, MCP/API access, scheduled/triggered agents, and credit-metered usage with approval before outbound are table stakes. Differentiators are proprietary data/signals, real autonomy with trustworthy guardrails, delivery where the user already is (messaging channels, mobile), human-plus-AI service models, and predictable pricing. The loudest complaints are about unpredictable credit costs, fully autonomous outbound that fails (AI SDR churn), hallucination/data-quality, missing basics in young CRMs, and tier gating at incumbents.

### Cited Findings
Table-stakes evidence (feature appears across many vendors):
- Auto-capture of email/calendar/meetings + AI summaries: Clarify (recorder all tiers), Monaco, Reevo (recording minutes), Zero, folk (call recording, email/calendar/WhatsApp sync), Rox (macOS meeting transcription), Twenty (Teams/Granola/Call Recorder apps) — [Clarify](https://www.clarify.ai/pricing); [Reevo](https://reevo.ai/pricing); [folk](https://www.folk.app/pricing); [Rox docs](https://docs.rox.com/development/llms.txt); [Twenty releases](https://twenty.com/releases)
- Meeting prep/briefs: Clarify (external meeting briefings, Jul 2026), Reevo, Rox, Affinity Ascend — [IndustryLens](https://industry-lens.com/reports/signal-spotlight-clarify); [Reevo blog](https://reevo.ai/blog/redefining-how-companies-go-to-market); [Affinity](https://support.affinity.co/whats-new)
- Built-in enrichment/prospect data: Clarify Lead Finder, Monaco database, Rox waterfall of 15 providers, Unify 1.1B people, Reevo credits, folk Find emails, Twenty PDL app — sources above
- MCP: Twenty, folk, Affinity, Rox, Reevo, Clarify agents, Zoho, HubSpot connectors — sources above
- Credit/action metering: Clarify, Rox, Unify, folk, Twenty, Reevo, HubSpot, Salesforce, Zoho — sources above
- Approval for external actions: Zero's explicit autonomous-internal/approval-external rule — [Zero](https://zero.inc/blog/ai-agents-crm-guardrails); HubSpot Breeze Assistant checks back with the user at each step — [AIMG](https://www.aimg.com/blog/hubspot-breeze-assistant-unbound-2026/)

Differentiator evidence:
- Proprietary data/signals bought, not built: Clarify bought Seam for datasets "that can't be reached with a simple search"; Zoom bought Common Room's signals — [IndustryLens](https://industry-lens.com/reports/signal-spotlight-clarify); [Zoom IR](https://investors.zoom.us/news-releases/news-release-details/zoom-acquire-common-room-bringing-buyer-intelligence-its-ai)
- Human-plus-AI service: Monaco's forward-deployed AEs; Clarify Enterprise "forward-deployed GTM partner" — [Paperclipped](https://www.paperclipped.de/en/blog/monaco-ai-native-crm-sales-platform/); [Clarify pricing](https://www.clarify.ai/pricing)
- Persistent per-account agents with memory (Actively) — [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/actively-series-b/)
- Warehouse-native/in-VPC (Rox) for enterprise — [Contrary](https://research.contrary.com/company/rox)
- Unlimited seats as a wedge vs seat-priced CRMs (Clarify, Rox Teams, Reevo) — [Clarify pricing](https://www.clarify.ai/pricing); [Rox pricing](https://www.rox.com/pricing)

User complaints:
- Credits as hidden/unpredictable cost (HubSpot): credits "easy to miss", don't roll over, features pause when exhausted; Customer Agent charged per conversation "even spam"; agents gated to Pro/Enterprise so real entry cost is the hub tier — [eesel.ai](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/is-hubspot-breeze-worth-it); [Default](https://www.default.com/post/hubspot-breeze-ai-review-and-pricing); [Resolve247](https://resolve247.ai/blog/hubspot-ai-agent-pricing/) (all third-party, some competitors)
- Credit burn on Clarify (competitor claim): meeting summaries at 30 credits each exhaust the free allowance; estimated ~$140/mo for three reps vs advertised entry price — [Coffee.ai blog (direct competitor, biased)](https://blog.coffee.ai/?p=1992). G2's top Clarify cons are "missing features" (8 reviews) and integration issues (4) — [G2 Clarify reviews](https://g2.com/products/clarify-crm/reviews)
- Skepticism of autonomy: Product Hunt commenter on Clarify — "most sales ops teams want AI suggestions, not AI decisions, especially on pipeline" — [Product Hunt (via search summary)](https://www.producthunt.com/p/clarify-6/clarify-7?page=4)
- AI SDR failure mode: TechCrunch (Mar 2025) reported 70–80% early churn at 11x (11x disputed, citing 79% retention); Artisan's CEO conceded first-gen AI SDRs had low reply rates, high churn and hallucination at launch; category churn of 50–70%/yr cited from UserGems; buyers moved to hybrid "AI drafts, human sends" — [11x guide summarising TechCrunch (biased)](https://www.11x.ai/guides/artisan-vs-aisdr-vs-11x); [Autobound buyer's guide](https://autobound.ai/blog/ai-sdr-buying-guide-2026); [Salesmotion](https://salesmotion.io/blog/ai-sdr-claims-scrutiny-what-buyers-should-verify)
- Hallucination/data quality: agents on messy CRM data (dupes, stale fields) use bad information — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/llm-crm-ai-integration-stack-sales-teams); Zero itself lists email-to-company mismatches and stale enrichment as known limits — [Zero](https://zero.inc/blog/ai-agents-crm-guardrails)
- Immaturity of challengers: Clarify described as "AI-native but young" with an agent that automates one pipeline; Zero notes a smaller ecosystem — [Breakcold (vendor)](https://www.breakcold.com/blog/crm-for-ai-agents); [Zero blog](https://zero.inc/blog/what-crm-do-startups-use)
- Unverifiable vendor claims: Monaco had no G2/Capterra reviews or case studies as of Mar 2026; HubSpot's "2.2x more leads" flagged as a claim to test — [Paperclipped](https://www.paperclipped.de/en/blog/monaco-ai-native-crm-sales-platform/); [AIMG](https://www.aimg.com/blog/hubspot-breeze-assistant-unbound-2026/)

### Inferences
Where an AI CRM with autonomous Bots (prospecting, outreach drafting with approval, morning briefs, scheduled routines, messaging the Bot over Telegram/SMS/Slack) stands, as of late 2026:
- Table stakes it must match: auto-capture of email/calendar/meetings; AI-maintained records; meeting prep + recaps; drafted follow-ups; built-in enrichment/lead finding; AI chat over the CRM; MCP + public API; scheduled/triggered agents; approval queue for anything customer-facing; activity/audit log of agent actions; a visible credit/usage meter with alerts and caps.
- Partly table stakes, partly differentiating: the morning brief. Meeting briefs and "always-on insights" are common (Rox Slack DMs, Clarify briefings, Affinity Ascend), but a single daily "here is your day, approve these" digest pushed to the owner is not a named feature at most vendors reviewed — moderate differentiator, easy to copy.
- Differentiators: (1) messaging the Bot over Telegram/SMS (and WhatsApp) — not offered by any notable vendor found; Slack is covered by Rox and others, so Slack alone is not a differentiator; (2) per-job "can do without asking" autonomy controls are close to Zero's model but more granular than most; (3) a non-tech SMB/owner-operator focus with flat, predictable pricing contrasts with credit-burn complaints; (4) vertical data integrations (e.g., government-contracting bids) mirror the "proprietary data" moat others are buying.
- Risks: "autonomous outbound" is now associated with AI-SDR churn and spam; marketing should lean on approval-first and quality, as Monaco and Zero do. Enrichment/signals quality is where well-funded competitors are spending acquisition money, so a thin enrichment layer is the weakest point versus Rox/Unify/Clarify-Seam.

### Gaps
- No direct Reddit/X threads found specifically reviewing Rox, Reevo, Monaco, Zero or Actively; sentiment evidence is mostly G2 aggregates, competitor blogs and Product Hunt comments.
- No source found quantifying how often users actually approve vs edit AI-drafted outreach in any product.
- Could not confirm whether any vendor ships SMS/Telegram/WhatsApp as an agent channel beyond small vertical tools ([mychatbot.app](https://mychatbot.app/ai-crm), [ainora.lt](https://ainora.lt/blog/telegram-ai-business-assistant)) and freelance builds.
